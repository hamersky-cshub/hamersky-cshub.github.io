Wise Web Wanderers is a free, open cybersecurity education toolkit for **upper elementary learners**. It gives teachers everything needed to run lessons on safe, responsible online behaviour — lesson plans, printable materials, short videos, browser-based challenges and games — **without requiring specialist IT knowledge**.

## 💡 Why this project matters

Children are online earlier and more often than ever. Schools need practical, age-appropriate cybersecurity materials that do not require specialist training to teach. Wise Web Wanderers addresses that gap with ready-to-use content designed for real classroom constraints.

## ✨ Key points

| | |
|---|---|
| 🏫 **Built for real classrooms** | Age-appropriate language and scenarios for upper elementary learners. |
| 🧭 **Complete learning journey** | 7 modules covering digital citizenship, attacker mindset, authentication, privacy, social engineering, malware and digital abuse. |
| 🙌 **Active learning** | Every module has an interactive challenge; five modules also have a game. |
| 🧑‍🏫 **Teacher-friendly** | Each module comes with a teaching guide, step-by-step activity plans and downloadable packages. Teach the full sequence or pick single parts. |

| 💸 **Free and open** | MIT-licensed — use, adapt and share the materials freely. |

## 🚀 Quick start for educators

1. **Open the [Learning Hub](https://kidcyproject.github.io/learning-hub/introduction/)** and choose your language in the top navigation.
2. **Pick a module** (see [the seven modules](#-the-seven-modules)). Beginners usually start with *Digital Citizenship*.
3. **Download the Teaching Guide** at the top of the module page — it explains every material and how to use it.
4. **Read the module's aim, objectives and outcomes** so you know what students should be able to do afterwards.
5. **Choose a part** and open it to see its goal, activity plan, materials and video.
6. **Click "Download Package"** to get everything for that part in one ZIP — print worksheets and cards in advance.
7. **Test the video, challenge and game** on the classroom device before the lesson.
8. **Teach!** Close with the module's interactive challenge (and game, where available) to consolidate learning.

## 🧩 How the toolkit is organised

Every module follows the same predictable structure, so once you know one, you know them all:

```text
Module  (e.g. Authentication)
├── 📘 Teaching Guide ............ context and instructions for every material
├── 🎯 Aim · Objectives · Outcomes
├── 📦 Part 1 … Part N ........... one lesson unit each
│     ├── Goal of the part
│     ├── Activity plan .......... Introduction → Main activity → Plenary & informal assessment
│     ├── Materials .............. worksheets, scenario/sorting cards, images, posters, checklists
│     └── 🎬 Video (most parts) .. with downloadable subtitles in 5 languages
├── 🕹️ Interactive Challenge ..... browser-based, no install needed
└── 🎮 Game (5 modules) .......... played in the Roblox app
```

**Recommended classroom flow:**

```text
Teaching Guide  →  Parts (content + activities + video)  →  Interactive Challenge  →  Game (where available)
   prepare              teach                                  practise                  reinforce
```

## 🎮 Cybersecurity games (Roblox)

Five modules include a game that reinforces the module's key ideas through play. Use them as a warm-up, a reward, a full lesson activity or an optional take-home activity.

| Module | Game | In short |
|---|---|---|
| Authentication | [Master Spell: Unlock the Vault](https://kidcyproject.github.io/learning-hub/authentication/game/) | Choose stronger passwords, track the right knight (2FA), craft a Master Spell |
| Data Privacy | [Secrets of the Pearl & Shell](https://kidcyproject.github.io/learning-hub/data-privacy/game/) | Sort public vs private data, review posts, clean up a digital footprint |
| Social Engineering | [Scam or Safe? Don't Fall for It!](https://kidcyproject.github.io/learning-hub/social-engineering/game/) | Jump across platforms by spotting phishing correctly |
| Malware | [Malware Madness Race](https://kidcyproject.github.io/learning-hub/malware/game/) | Race three laps collecting protections and avoiding threats |
| Digital Abuse | [Kindness Quest: Save the City](https://kidcyproject.github.io/learning-hub/digital-abuse/game/) | Resolve conflicts about cyberbullying and deepfakes |

> [!WARNING]
> **Games require the Roblox app.** Clicking *Play* opens the game in Roblox; if it isn't installed, students are prompted to download it. Before class, check that:
> - Roblox is allowed by your school's IT and device policy,
> - the app is installed on classroom devices and the game opens,
> - you know how students will sign in (Roblox accounts are subject to Roblox's own terms and age requirements).
>
> If Roblox isn't an option, the **interactive challenges cover every module** and need only a browser.

## 📦 Downloading materials

You can download at whichever level suits you:

| Download | Where to find it | Contains |
|---|---|---|
| **Teaching Guide** | Top of each module page | Guidance for every material in the module |
| **Download All Materials** | Module page | Every part package of the module in one ZIP |
| **Download Package** | Each part | Worksheets, cards, images and video for that part |
| **Single material** | Each part's material list | One worksheet / card set / image |
| **Video + subtitles** | Under each video | The MP4 and subtitle files (`.vtt`) in all 5 languages |

> Downloaded videos with subtitles let you teach **offline** — handy if your classroom internet is unreliable. Most video players (e.g. VLC) can load `.vtt` subtitle files.

**Material types you will encounter:** worksheets, scenario cards, sorting cards, role cards, game cards, images and posters, schemas, checklists, comic stories, readings and situation assessments — most are designed to be **printed** for group or individual work.

## 🗓️ Planning your teaching

Each part is a **self-contained lesson unit** with its own activity plan (typically: *Introduction → main activity → Plenary: summary and informal assessment*). Use the teaching guide to decide how much time each part needs for your class.

**Option A — Full programme (recommended).** Teach all 7 modules in order (31 parts). The sequence builds from general digital citizenship to specific threats:

```text
1 Digital Citizenship → 2 Attacker Perspective → 3 Authentication → 4 Data Privacy
                     → 5 Social Engineering → 6 Malware → 7 Digital Abuse
```

**Option B — Single module.** Pick one topic that fits your curriculum or a current issue in class (e.g. *Social Engineering* after a phishing incident).

**Option C — Individual parts.** Drop a single part into an existing ICT, citizenship or personal-development lesson.

**Option D — Challenge or game only.** Use a challenge as a quick formative check, an end-of-term review, or a homework task.

## ✅ Classroom checklist

**Before the lesson**
- [ ] Read the module's teaching guide and the part's activity plan
- [ ] Print the worksheets and cut out any cards
- [ ] Play the video on the classroom screen (or download it for offline use) and turn on subtitles in your language
- [ ] Try the challenge yourself; for games, check that Roblox works on the devices

**Equipment**
- 🖥️ Projector or large screen with speakers — for videos and whole-class discussion
- 💻 Computers or tablets with a modern web browser — for challenges (individually or in pairs)
- 🖨️ Printer — for worksheets and cards
- 🎮 Roblox app — only for the five games

**After the lesson**
- [ ] Use the plenary and the challenge results for informal assessment against the "I can…" outcomes
- [ ] Share your feedback with the project team (see [Collaboration](#-collaboration--feedback))

## ❓ FAQ

<details>
<summary><b>Do I need a technical or cybersecurity background?</b></summary>

No. The materials are written for teachers without specialist training. The teaching guide and activity plans walk you through each step.
</details>

<details>
<summary><b>What age group is this for?</b></summary>

Upper elementary learners. Adapt the pace and depth to your class — the modular structure makes it easy to shorten or extend.
</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Do students need accounts or to install anything?</b></summary>

Not for the lessons, videos or challenges — only a web browser. The games need the Roblox app.
</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Is it free? Can I adapt the materials?</b></summary>

Yes. Everything is free and released under the MIT License, so you may use, adapt and share it.
</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Can I use the materials without an internet connection?</b></summary>

Yes — download the part packages and videos (with subtitles) in advance. Challenges and games need an internet connection.
</details>
